Make the work visible.

Mail, documents, tasks, and calendar views belong to the same daily workflow. The design challenge was to bring these pieces together in a workspace for lawyers.

My contribution covered competitor analysis, mood boards, user flows, low-fidelity concepts, feature design, and visual foundations. I worked with the development lead and product manager to define the MVP scope.

Research with
evidence in view.

The goal was to make AI research feel human—not just put a text box on screen and call it done. I designed thoughtful microinteractions that make source selection, research progress, and citations visible, while keeping follow-up and recovery close at hand. Together, these details are intended to build trust by giving users more context and control as they review an answer.

I captured the key states, interaction rules, and the boundary between the simulated prototype and a future implementation in a design handoff for stakeholder and developer review.

Give documents
a clear home.

The document workspace brings folders, file details, and access information into a list that can be scanned quickly.

The design makes file type, update date, sharing, and actions visible while keeping the same navigation as the rest of the product.

Daily essentials before advanced features.

We wanted Google Drive-like search and tagging, but both required deep indexing work beyond the available development time.

I created design options with clear pros and cons and presented them to the development lead and product manager. Together, we selected an MVP focused on users’ essential daily document workflows.

Prioritize
Core document workflows that address users’ day-to-day needs within the available development time.
Defer
Advanced search and tagging, rather than expanding the MVP with indexing-heavy functionality.

Decision: We agreed on a focused MVP scope, balancing everyday usefulness with technical feasibility.

Current status: QA is ongoing ahead of final release. A release date has not been set.

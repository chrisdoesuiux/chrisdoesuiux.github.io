A shared foundation
for design and code.

Figma files and coded components had drifted apart. Without documented guidelines, engineers had to interpret design intent again for each feature.

I owned both the design system and Storybook, auditing Figma files and CSS and building the shared foundation from scratch. I documented component variants, states, and usage guidance, and managed and mentored two junior designers through design reviews.

15%

Less development time per task.

Development time per task decreased by 15% within the quarter after introducing the design system and Storybook.

Aligning the team around trade-offs.

A complex data-table redesign brought three requirements into tension: Product wanted maximum data density, Engineering flagged DOM performance issues, and Design required WCAG accessibility standards.

I facilitated an alignment session and partnered with a frontend lead to prototype progressive disclosure directly in code, using summary rows and a side panel.

Product
Keep high-value data in the summary rows and move secondary columns into a drawer, avoiding horizontal scrolling.
Engineering
Use virtualized scrolling for performance, supported by standardized row heights and design tokens.
Accessibility
Replace complex custom micro-interactions with standard ARIA patterns, simplifying implementation while preserving accessibility requirements.

Outcome: Engineering avoided technical debt and met performance benchmarks. Product delivered high-value data access on schedule, while Design maintained the accessibility requirements. Standard ARIA patterns also reduced frontend implementation time.

Give writing
the space it needs.

A user survey showed that posts needed room for more than 200 characters. The dialog-first pattern made longer writing difficult, especially on mobile.

I analysed the survey data and redesigned the posting experience, moving long-form content to standalone pages. The writing layout became more spacious and better suited to touch.

25%

Higher message posting rate.

The message posting rate improved by 25% after the research-led UI/UX improvements.

Make research
part of the rhythm.

Research needed a repeatable structure so the team could make decisions with confidence, rather than rely on occasional feedback.

I introduced quarterly focus groups with five target users, combining usability testing and A/B testing. I also partnered with a data engineer on an analytics dashboard.

Listen regularly

Scheduled user sessions gave the team a consistent way to understand needs and test changes.

Connect the evidence

Click-through rate, onboarding conversion, and task completion complemented the qualitative feedback.

Inform decisions

User sessions and live data became inputs to design decisions, alongside the shared component system.
